# Model Card: Lapse Prediction

## Purpose

Teaching artifact for modelling a lapse decision and a payment-timing
distribution as **one** ordered target rather than two chained models, plus the
data discipline that matters more than the algorithm choice. **Not a production
underwriting or retention model, and not fit for decisions about real
policyholders.**

## Data

Synthetic. Generated by `src/lapse_prediction/data/generate.py` as a renewal
ledger with one row per policy-due-date. No real policies, no customer records,
no personal data.

The generator contains a latent per-policy `discipline` term, bimodal payment
timing (a spike at day 0 and another near the grace deadline), a revival tail
for premiums arriving months late, and imbalanced outcomes at roughly a 10%
lapse rate.

`data/schema.py` validates the contract at ingest, so swapping the generator
for a warehouse query fails loudly on a shape change instead of degrading the
model quietly.

## Target

Days from due date to premium receipt, cut into five ordered buckets:

| bucket | meaning |
|---|---|
| `d0_7`, `d8_15`, `d16_30`, `d31_grace` | paid within the 45-day grace period |
| `lapsed` | no premium received inside grace |

`P(lapse)` is the last cell of the distribution. Expected days is derived from
the shape of the first four. The target is never regressed on raw days, because
the distribution is skewed and spiky and a conditional mean falls in a gap where
almost no mass sits.

## Models

Eleven families behind one interface (`models/zoo.py`). The ones that carry the
argument:

| model | what it is | why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| `ordinal_chain` | binary model per cumulative edge, differenced CDF | **the recommendation** |
| `hurdle_2stage` | classifier then regression on paid rows | the two-stage baseline, kept to be measured |
| `discrete_hazard` | discrete-time survival | the right shape once revivals matter |
| `logit` | multinomial logistic regression | the permanent challenger |
| `cox_ph`, `xgb_aft` | survival alternatives | measured, not assumed |

## Measured results (out-of-time split, 5,424 validation rows, 10.2% lapse)

| model | lapse PR-AUC | AUC | Brier | mlogloss | days MAE | capture@20% | fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| blend(RF+ordinal) | 0.3833 | 0.8163 | 0.0761 | 1.1040 | 13.33 | 59.8% | n/a |
| random_forest | 0.3831 | 0.8161 | 0.0762 | 1.1047 | 13.38 | 58.2% | 14s |
| **ordinal_chain** | 0.3804 | 0.8151 | 0.0761 | 1.1273 | 13.28 | 59.1% | 12s |
| logit | 0.3729 | 0.8178 | 0.0762 | 1.1093 | 13.34 | 59.8% | 2s |
| hurdle_2stage | 0.3576 | 0.8017 | 0.0780 | 1.1347 | 13.24 | 58.3% | 46s |
| prior (baseline) | 0.1018 | 0.5000 | 0.0915 | 1.2651 | 16.16 | 19.2% | 0s |

Every legitimate model sits in AUC 0.802 to 0.818. Best-to-worst across real
models is 0.027 PR-AUC; any of them to the prior baseline is 0.28.

## Known limitations

- **The ranking is nearly flat across algorithms, and that is the finding.**
  Multinomial logistic regression posts the highest AUC in the table and fits in
  two seconds. Reading this card as evidence that the ordinal chain is
  materially more accurate would be reading it backwards; it is recommended for
  the shape of its output, not for a score.
- **The two-stage hurdle finished 10th of 11.** It is included so the comparison
  is measured rather than asserted, but it is a baseline, not an option.
- **`discrete_hazard` is weak inside the grace window**, because weekly periods
  read the day-45 edge at day 42. Its real job is the revival horizon, where it
  is well calibrated at every checkpoint.
- **All numbers come from synthetic data with a known generative process.** Real
  books show weaker payment-history signal and more drift. The out-of-time gap
  is the first thing to check after porting.
- **The model is not production-validated.** There is no drift monitoring, no
  fairness or model-risk review, and unseen categorical levels currently score
  without complaining.
- **The engineering around it is production-shaped**: versioned registry, model
  cards, ingest contract, release gate, leakage tests. Read that as the
  scaffolding being ready and the model not being.

## The failures that cost more than the algorithm

1. **Leakage.** All history is built with `groupby.shift(1)`;
   `assert_no_leakage()` aborts the run if a forbidden column reaches the
   matrix; `tests/test_leakage.py` tampers with an outcome and asserts no
   feature moves.
2. **Cohort maturity.** Training on dues whose grace has not elapsed relabels
   not-yet-paid as lapsed. `labels.mature()` drops them.
3. **Out-of-time validation.** Split by due-date cohort, never randomly. Early
   stopping reads the TEST cohort so VALID stays untouched.
4. **Calibration.** Isotonic recalibration is fitted on a cohort the model never
   trained on, with the remaining bucket mass renormalised so rows still sum
   to 1.

## Release gate

`evaluation.report.gate()` fails the training pipeline with a non-zero exit on a
PR-AUC, Brier or monotonicity regression, so a worse model cannot ship silently.

## Reproducibility

Fixed seeds throughout. `pytest` (57 tests) covers leakage, schema, the split,
model contracts, the registry, serving, and an end-to-end pipeline run. Tests
write nothing into the project's `data/` directory.
