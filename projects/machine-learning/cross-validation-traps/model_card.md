# Model Card: Cross-Validation Trap Lab

## Purpose

Teaching artifact for a single question that is normally answered by assertion:
when two validation schemes disagree, **which one is right, and by how much?**
Every scheme here is scored against a holdout that was constructed to be what
deployment actually is. **Not a churn model, and not fit for any decision about
real customers.**

The model itself is uninteresting on purpose. Nothing in this project is a claim
about gradient boosting or logistic regression; every number is a difference
between validation procedures applied to identical data.

## Data

Synthetic. Generated by `src/cv_traps/data/generate.py` as a subscription panel
with one row per customer-period. No real customers, no personal data.

| property | value |
|---|---|
| rows | 15,211 |
| customers | 2,600 |
| periods | 12 |
| features | 9 real, 150 pure noise |
| churn rate | 27.7% |
| rows per customer | 5.85 average |

Four structures are planted deliberately, each switchable to off:

| structure | dial | what it creates |
|---|---|---|
| latent per-customer effect | `group_effect: 2.50` | the group trap |
| drifting coefficients and base rate | `drift: 0.90` | the time trap |
| 150 pure-noise columns | `n_noise: 150` | the preprocessing and selection traps |
| label noise | `label_noise: 0.05` | candidates close enough that ranking is luck |

`data/schema.py` validates the contract at ingest, including that the
customer-level columns really are constant within a customer, because the group
trap depends on it.

## The truth

Defined once, in `evaluation/truth.py`, and used by every experiment:

> a model fitted on development, evaluated on **unseen customers** in
> **future periods**

Development is periods 0 to 8 for 1,649 customers (7,030 rows). The holdout is
periods 9 to 11 for 571 customers held out entirely (1,507 rows). No customer
appears on both sides, and the split asserts it.

Both conditions matter. Future periods alone would still let the model recognise
returning customers; unseen customers alone would still validate against a world
that had already happened.

## Measured results

### The four biased traps, ranked

| trap | naive CV | careful CV | truth | naive optimism | careful optimism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| grouped | 0.7430 | 0.7061 | 0.7165 | **+0.0265** | -0.0103 |
| temporal | 0.7430 | 0.6896 | 0.7165 | **+0.0265** | -0.0269 |
| preprocessing | 0.7499 | 0.7419 | 0.7637 | -0.0138 | -0.0218 |
| selection | 0.7453 | 0.7434 | 0.7682 | -0.0229 | -0.0248 |

### Two families, separated by how they respond to sample size

| development rows | selection leakage | winner's curse | customer leakage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 205 | 0.1800 | 0.0461 | 0.0198 |
| 412 | 0.1270 | 0.0345 | 0.0475 |
| 823 | 0.0545 | 0.0207 | 0.0505 |
| 1,996 | 0.0261 | 0.0066 | 0.0588 |
| 6,992 | 0.0075 | 0.0023 | 0.0375 |

The statistical traps fall by more than an order of magnitude. The structural
one does not fall at all.

### Variance, which is not a bias

20 reshuffles of the same grouped 5-fold CV:

| model | mean | std | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (C=1.0) | 0.7490 | 0.0007 | 0.0025 |
| B (C=0.1) | 0.7489 | 0.0007 | 0.0024 |

The gap between the models is 0.0001, which is a twenty-fifth of the spread
within either one. A beat B in 16 of 20 repeats: consistent in direction,
negligible in size.

## Known limitations

- **No careful scheme lands on the truth.** GroupKFold sits at -0.0103 and
  grouped forward chaining at -0.0269. Reading this card as "use grouped folds
  and your number is correct" is reading it backwards. The correct schemes are
  conservative, not accurate, and the honest report is a range.
- **Part of every optimism figure is not leakage.** CV trains on four fifths of
  development while the truth model trains on all of it, so a small pessimistic
  component is baked into each estimate. The leak-specific numbers, which are
  train-size matched, are the naive-minus-careful differences, and those are
  what the sample-size tables report.
- **Drift changes difficulty as well as transferability.** Later periods carry a
  stronger `days_since_login` coefficient, so raising `drift` makes the holdout
  easier and can flip the sign of the measured optimism. At `drift: 3.0` naive
  KFold reads -0.020. This is why the default sits at 0.90 and why the temporal
  trap is reported as a comparison between schemes rather than as a headline.
- **The temporal effect is small on its own.** Forward chaining removes 28,120
  future training rows and recovers only about half the error, because the
  remaining half is customer leakage that time-ordering does not touch.
- **Group leakage is noisy across sample sizes** (0.0198 to 0.0588). The claim
  supported by the data is that it does not systematically shrink, not that it
  is constant.
- **All numbers come from one synthetic panel with one seed.** They are stable
  under reseeding in direction, not in the third decimal.
- **The model is not production-validated.** It is not a model of anything.

## The controls

A project that only ever shows leakage looks like a project that cannot tell
leakage from an artefact of the method. Two controls exist for that reason:

1. `python run.py data --group-effect 0.0 --drift 0.0` rebuilds the panel with
   both structures removed. The gaps should collapse.
2. `tests/test_generator.py::test_turning_the_group_effect_off_removes_the_group_leak`
   asserts it: with no latent customer effect, stratified and grouped folds
   agree within 0.02.

Without these, "grouped CV scores lower" would be equally consistent with
grouped folds simply training on fewer distinct customers.

## Reproducibility

Fixed seeds throughout. `pytest` (36 tests) covers the generator, all five
splitters, each trap's direction, the schema, the truth split, and the CLI.
Tests write nothing into the project's `data/` directory.

`conf/config.yaml` carries every threshold. The cache is guarded by a sidecar
manifest, so a panel built at other settings is detected and regenerated instead
of being read in silence.
