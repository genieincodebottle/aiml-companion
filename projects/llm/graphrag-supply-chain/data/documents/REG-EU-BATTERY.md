# Regulatory Notice: EU Battery Regulation Due Diligence

Issued by: Northwind Regulatory Affairs
Applies to: products placed on the EU market containing an industrial or
portable battery

## What the regulation requires

The EU Battery Regulation introduces supply chain due diligence obligations on
economic operators placing batteries on the EU market. The obligations that
affect Northwind directly are:

1. A documented due diligence policy covering cobalt, natural graphite, lithium
   and nickel in the battery supply chain.
2. Identification and assessment of adverse impacts in that supply chain,
   including at upstream stages the operator does not purchase from directly.
3. Third-party verification of the due diligence policy.
4. A carbon footprint declaration for the battery, calculated per the
   prescribed methodology.

## What this means for Northwind

The obligation attaches to the LI-18650 battery pack. Northwind must be able
to identify not only who assembles the pack but who supplies the active
materials in it, because obligation 2 explicitly reaches beyond the direct
supplier relationship.

Our current position is partial. We can name the pack assemblers. Naming the
cathode material producer behind each assembler required the deep-tier mapping
exercise rather than a routine ERP query, and the mapping for anode graphite
is not complete.

## Carbon footprint declaration

The declaration must reflect the actual pack supplied. Where packs from
different assemblers carry materially different embodied carbon, using a single
blended figure is not compliant if the pack origin is known at the point of
sale.

## Action

Regulatory Affairs requires, from the supply risk team, a documented material
chain for the LI-18650 covering all pack assemblers and their cathode material
sources, by 30 September 2026.
